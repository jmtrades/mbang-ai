# The programme's method (notes for the coach)

Paraphrased notes from the programme owner's masterclass ("recurring high-ticket model") and group coaching calls
(client acquisition and follow-up). Used when a student's question isn't answered directly by a course lesson. These
are notes in our own words, not quotes.

## Core philosophy
- Recurring high-ticket: few clients, high touch, high price. One big client outweighs many small ones; put the same
  energy into high-ticket prospects only.
- Results and fulfilment over chasing volume. Everyone online shouts "get more clients"; very few show results. Back
  end (retention, upsells) can be a big share of revenue.
- Sell the outcome, not the deliverables. Price signals who it's for.
- Standards and speed: offer owners stay stuck because they're slow, inconsistent and scared of the next risk; they
  contract and try to protect what they have. Serious operators put money where their mouth is, hire A-players and
  understand risk. Say this plainly; people protect their identity and rise to the standard you set.
- Honesty over hype: never sell dreams. "Here's the plan, here's what it takes, here's the risk. Are you in, yes or no?"
  Invite blunt feedback: "If we're full of it, tell us."

## The money math (reverse-engineering the goal)
- Start from the monthly revenue goal. Goal ÷ price = clients needed. Clients ÷ close rate = sales calls. Calls ÷ show
  rate = booked calls. Booked calls × cost per booked call = ad spend needed.
- Use the client's real current stats and keep them conservative; they could be better, but plan on reality.
- Worked example style: at $2,500 a client, $100K/month needs 40 clients, about 200 calls, 400 booked calls, and a
  large ad budget. At $10,000 a client it's 10 clients, about 50 calls, 100 booked calls: suddenly realistic. Raising
  the price is the lever that makes the plan possible.
- The same math applies to webinars, funnels, outreach messages and video pitches: just different steps and stats.
  Work out how many messages, pitches or dollars you are away from the goal, and the daily targets become obvious.
- Find the first, easiest statistic to double and go after that.

## Getting a client to spend more on ads
- You don't "convince" anyone; you show them the math. Build their financial model with their own numbers, show what
  their current spend can reach and what the goal actually needs. The gap makes the case.
- Under-funding is a classic mistake: spending a fraction of what a launch or webinar needs and then wondering why
  results stall. Get the client to spend what the plan requires.
- Push them to risk more, but not to break the bank. Tell them exactly how much spend the goal needs and why.
- Track every lead's source (Instagram, YouTube, ads, email, community) through to booked calls, closes and cash, and
  double down on what converts. When the data shows which channel pays, scaling spend stops being a debate.
- Frame risk honestly: no guarantees, but a clear plan, clear targets and transparency.

## Sales calls and closing
- No script needed: on the call, show the prospect their numbers, the financial model and the plan to reach their
  goal. That certainty sells.
- On a verbal yes, ask for a small refundable deposit right then ("this is how we work; it's a standard we hold").
  Don't let them off the hook.
- Pre-sell assets do the heavy lifting: a video that pre-handles objections and walks through the process, sent before
  the call, builds trust.
- B2C buyers: high urgency, little money, impulsive. B2B or wealthy buyers: money, low urgency, longer cycles. Build
  urgency with pre-sell assets, nurture and showing what the bottleneck is costing them.

## Client acquisition and follow-up
- Pipeline videos: 10-25 minute YouTube videos made about a specific prospect's bottleneck, sent to them directly
  ("you inspired this one"). For the first six months YouTube is nurture for the pipeline, not reach. Pitch at the
  start of the video; free resource plus paid options in the description.
- Keep production simple: screen or whiteboard, quick edits, clean thumbnails with your face, titles that spark
  curiosity or anchor a number.
- Be everywhere: daily short clips with subtitles and an intentional title (often a negative anchor, the mechanism,
  or the problem/solution). Always give something away: "comment KEYWORD" on screen and on the first line of the
  caption.
- On-the-spot clips: when something happens on a call or a launch, film it right away. Real emotion and story travel
  furthest.
- The washing machine: everyone who engages gets a conversation, goes into the CRM and keeps receiving useful videos,
  clips and resources about their exact problem until they're ready. Follow up like a boss by sending value, never
  "any update?". Aim for at least weekly; daily is better.
- Know the ideal client in detail (behaviour, speed, standards) and speak only to them in content; you attract who you
  describe.
- Cold video pitches to high-ticket prospects: position yourself as the expert first with the process or a case study
  (you can explain the exact actions behind a result), then sell. Referrals from a community are a strong early
  source.

## Delivery
- Weekly rhythm with clients: diagnose the bottleneck, fix the team (replace C-players with A-players), fine-tune the
  sales process (review calls, secret-shop, pre-selling assets), implement KPIs, and push for calculated risk.
- When the client's team isn't closing qualified leads, step in as a trainer and hold them accountable to the numbers.
